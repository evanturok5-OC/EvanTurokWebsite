Movie Review - All the President’s Men Written By Evan Turok Alan J. Pakula's 1976 mystery thriller All the President's Men is a show of excellence in every aspect that goes into a film. From a realistic yet funny script that continues to build upon itself throughout, to acting that is natural and engaging, and, of course, award-winning production design, All the President's Men holds a standard that has been proven so hard to maintain. This film is set fresh off events of the Watergate scandal, an incident in which one political party is caught attempting to illegally spy on and record the opposing party. This had devastating effects on the US and is cited as ruining the public’s trust for their elected officials. We follow a pair of newspaper journalists who are set on finding the truth and exposing the story to the public. Dustin Hoffman and Robbert Reford have a chemistry impossible to replicate. They are able to bounce off one another with a casual grace, paired with a tightly written, suspenseful, and smart script, you can't help but be kept in the flow of the story, totally immersed, never given a chance to catch your breath. Hoffman especially is very funny, which helps to cut through the decently complicated nature of the constantly unraveling political mystery. A technically great film. The uses of different camera techniques made sure the entire frame was in action. Never was the background blurred completely, as seen in many modern films; instead, there was a deliberate focus, literally and figuratively, on the background as well as the foreground, ensuring each frame was lively, filled with people and activity that almost surrounded my viewing at the beautiful Astor Theater into a 360-degree experience, inviting you into the adventure and creating an environment rather than simply a scene. For such a complex real-life situation, All the President's Men did a great job of telling a story in a clear and entertaining fashion. A film that reminds you of just how well-crafted, surprisingly funny, and engaging it is, as much as it reminds you of the name Bob Woodward.
