Movie Review – Star Wars: The Mandalorian and Grogu Written by Evan Turok Too many times, my brother and I turned to each other in silent disbelief, and shared a knowing smirk of hopelessness, directed at the soulless, senseless trainwreck of a film that was Jon Favreau's ‘The Mandalorian and Grogu,’ A good fantasy or sci-fi movie should leave you with a sense of wonder and inspire you to let your imagination run wild with the possibilities of the world you have paid your $18 to enter. So, with that said, I give full credit to ‘The Mandalorian and Grogu’ for making me wish I had the powers of the Force, if only for the ability to force my eyes open during the tedious two hours-plus of this mind-numbing film. From the story to the dialogue, the writers served up a course each of dull predictability and cringe-inducing conversation. The character of the ‘Mandalorian’ wears a helmet for almost the entire film, so it made complete sense to learn that during the filming of these scenes, so-called ‘lead actor’ Pedro Pascal was not the one inside the armor. Instead, he only provided voice-overs for the film. That alone should be enough to show you the level of passion put in by Pascal, which further reflects in his consistently monotone and blasé performance. A poorly paced film that sadly slammed to a grinding halt with any scene featuring the little green Grogu and his occasionally cute, but always dull shenanigans. Ludwig Göransson has quickly become the most sought-after composer in Hollywood. He provided The Mandalorian and Grogu with a fantastic, futuristic space-western soundtrack that was well and truly above the standard of the film as a whole. To pair with the music was a handful of exciting, explosion-filled action scenes that are well shot and genuinely exciting whether you are a Star Wars fan or not. There was also a funny and random voice cameo from the great director Martin Scorsese, which was unexpectedly fitting for this month's reviews. If you are headed to the theaters for this film, try to treat it as a lackluster action movie rather than an extremely lackluster Star Wars movie.
