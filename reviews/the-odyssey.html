Movie review - The Odyssey Written by Evan Turok There was so much anticipation for Christopher Nolan's The Odyssey I thought it could never meet the astronomical expectations. Then it did. The Odyssey is the story of the mighty Greek warrior Odysseus' journey home from the Battle of Troy, a furious war won by the use of the famous Trojan horse. His quest home is met time and time again by an array of mythical beasts, creatures, witches, and the wrath of the gods themselves. Christopher Nolan delivers on every promise, an in-your-face story that shows every last detail. Whether it was bloody battles, the grim realities of the 40 men sealed in a wooden horse for days, or the twisted intricacies of a magical transformation, you see it all. As much of a visual feast as it was, the immersion reached its peak with the use of immaculate sound design. A specific scene comes to mind in which the point-of-view character blocks his ears with wax to avoid hearing the dangerously deceptive siren song. At first, one half of the cinema was dulled and then, separately, the other half. Soon, all you could hear was the gentle wind and a lulling horn amidst the chaos. It was a surreal experience. Due to the nature of the original story, there was a likelihood for this film to become an extended, repetitive montage, but the smart writing gave us a journey in which almost every line and action circles back and reveals itself to have more emotional depth than you first assume. Unfortunately, the music for this film lacked those iconic scores associated with past Nolan films. Although it got the job done, The Odyssey lacked a powerful composition to push it over the edge. The Odyssey is the best film in the last 12 months and demands to be watched in the best format possible. If you have a chance, take advantage of the fact that we live in the city with the largest screen on the face of the Earth, watch it in IMAX Melbourne, and enjoy the experience as it was intended.
