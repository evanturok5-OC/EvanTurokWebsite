Movie Review - Coyote vs. ACME Written By Evan Turok Breathing new life into classic characters is always a delicate task. Walking the tightrope of invitation and homage is something director Dave Green pulled off flawlessly and left us with perhaps the most fun movie of the year. Having had enough of ACME’s faulty gadgets constantly stifling him in his endless quest to catch the Road Runner, Looney Tunes character Wile E. Coyote sets out to sue the ACME Corporation. Represented by lawyer Kevin Avery (Will Forte), the pair set out to prove malicious intent behind the megacorporation’s malfunctioning products, such as rocket-powered roller skates and dehydrated boulders (Just add water!). What follows is a hilarious investigation and trial that features all the classic characters I hoped to see. Shot as a live-action cartoon in the style of 1988’s Who Framed Roger Rabbit, this film is a machine gun of comedy, firing off joke after joke, only pausing to deliver some shockingly heartfelt moments. When animation interacts with real actors and objects, it can often look out of place, but the time and passion put into the animation created such a strangely natural dynamic that never took me out of the film. Unfortunately, the original human characters are almost entirely forgettable, lacking any sort of personal touch or individuality. Considering Forte's character was front and center throughout the entirety of the film, it would have been nice to be given an opportunity to build a connection with the character. Some simple character additions would have taken the experiences to a new level. Upon shooting, Warner Brothers shelved this film for three years in an effort to claim it as a tax write-off. Thanks to the outcry of fans, cast, and crew, it was finally released to the public, and I'm so glad it is. If you are looking for an excuse to get back to the cinema, especially with the family, Coyote vs. ACME will have a little something for everyone and leave you with just a little more than your average family comedy. 3.5/5
