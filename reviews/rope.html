My review

Movie Review- Rope Written by Evan Turok Alfred Hitchcock is remembered for classics like ‘Psycho’ and ‘Rear Window’, while forgotten gems can slip through the cracks. This is why I’m calling ‘Rope’ (1948) Hitchcock’s most underrated film. We jump straight into the action where Brandon Shaw (John Dall) and Philip Morgan (Farley Granger) strangle a man named David to death in their apartment with a length of rope. The body is hidden in a chest and placed in the middle of the living room. Quickly, we learn that a party will be taking place in the very same apartment. A series of charismatically acted guests soon enter the home, each with a distinct personality and purpose, but only one guest named Rupert (James Stewart) suspects something is amiss when David fails to show up to the party, although some can argue David has been there all along. The apartment is where we stay throughout the whole movie, or more specifically, the living room. This limited set provides Hitchcock with the ability to try something revolutionary for the time: making a ‘one shot’ film. Contrary to the name, a ‘one shot’ movie is actually a full-length feature that is made up of long takes and a few hidden cuts throughout, giving the illusion of one continuous take. These hidden cuts can be seen with a keen eye anytime the screen is completely black — be this when an actor walks in front of the camera or for a split second when the lights go out. Modern films such as ‘1917’ (2019) and ‘Birdman’ (2014) may have perfected the ‘one shot’ technique, but ‘Rope’ revolutionized it seven decades earlier. Admittedly, some portions, specifically in the middle of the film, can drag on and focus on somewhat unrelated side characters whose personal motivations have almost nothing to do with the story at hand. That being said, Rope is a very well-written and perfectly directed thriller packed with sharp dialogue that keeps you on the edge of your seat for the majority of the film. Acting from the effortlessly talented James Stewart was a highlight that really… tied this film together. Overall, I give Rope (1948) 3.5/5.
