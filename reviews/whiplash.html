Movie review - ‘Whiplash’ Written by Evan Turok Damian Chazelle’s Whiplash (2014) strikes a chord, literally and figuratively. This intense and gripping, yet charming drama follows a 19-year-old named Andrew Neiman (Miles Teller) and his passion for drumming. He finds his calling in the form of the most prestigious jazz bands in the country, headed by the aggressive perfectionist Terence Fletcher (J.K. Simmons). Our story kicks off when Neiman is seen playing his drums by Fletcher and is invited, in that signature demanding way, to play in his expert-level band. Kicking off the psychological abuse for Neiman, who is unknowingly called into practice three hours early. As it turns out, mental and physical abuse is commonplace in Fletcher’s band. Andrew Neiman comes to learn that if his dream to be the best is to come to fruition, he must, for better or worse, move on from the life he currently lives, embrace obsession and commit to the goal he has put his blood sweat and tears into. The highlight of this incredible film has to be the soundtrack. Swinging jazz music serenades the audience, who would otherwise close their eyes to fully immerse themselves in the music if it weren’t for cinematography that kept pace like a drummer playing double time. Many cuts are expertly timed to sync up with the rhythmic jazz which delivered some fantastic sequences. Watching a film this well put together is a delight, and it’s not hard to see why two of Whiplash’s three Academy Awards were for sound mixing and editing. The strong language and aggressive nature of Mr. Fletcher have earned Whiplash an MA rating, but by no means should this deter a viewer from enjoying such a visual and auditory masterpiece. For me, the most important thing about any movie is that it’s entertaining, and keeping this film to a digestible hour-forty Damian Chzelle has ensured each scene has the impact of a bass drum. I recommend you watch it immediately — a mix of amazing acting from J.K. Simmons, a superb soundtrack, and excellent editing leaves me no choice but to give this movie an almost perfect 4.5/5.
