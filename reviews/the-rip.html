Movie Review - The Rip Written by Evan Turok The cinema is a magical place. Complete strangers come together with only one thing in common; you have agreed to put your phones down and engage in the film in front of you. Joe Carnahan’s new Netflix original film The Rip (2026) makes me crave warm buttery popcorn and the light of a projector the way someone who has just eaten a hot chili craves milk. The Rip follows a group of Miami police officers led by lieutenant Dane Dumars (Matt Damon) and J.D. Byrne (Ben Affleck) who discover twenty million dollars stashed in the wall of a cartel safe house during a routine seizure of illegal assets, or in police terms, a rip. As trust frays between the team, Dumars and Byrne begin to suspect that in their group of cops may be a rat who is working for the cartels. The standard drug bust quickly becomes a battle for self-preservation, and the vulnerable cash begins to look enticing to the officers. The Rip is lazy. Amazingly, the action scenes lack realism and entertainment at the same time. Story beats play out with simplistic, almost dumb convenience. I am almost grateful the entire film is engulfed in a dark, blue haze, making everything barely visible. Thank goodness for the charisma of Affleck and Damon, who just barely drag this movie to a watchable standard. I wish with all my heart that the reason for the artistic failure of The Rip came down to bad writing, a lack of ambition, and creativity, but the reality is much worse; The Rip was made this way on purpose. The phenomenon of ‘second-screen viewing’ is where writers, directors, and producers make a movie with the assumption that the audience will be watching it while on their phones. The film becomes simplistic and repeats important dialogue multiple times so that anyone not paying attention can be caught up with the plot. This direct-to-streaming movie was literally intended to be background noise, and it shows. The Rip can Rest In Peace in the cavernous abyss of the Netflix archives, far away from what makes cinema the most beautiful, all-encompassing art form on the earth today.
