My review

Movie review - The Player Written By Evan Turok 1992’s ‘The Player’ features Tim Robbins playing Griffin Mill, a studio executive in charge of separating the thousands of yearly film ideas from the mere twelve that will be produced. With an apathetic indifference to the countless writers he rejects on a daily basis, it becomes impossible to know which of them has begun to send death threats his way. What follows is a witty and tense comedy thriller supported by technical filmmaking excellence. Griffin Mill is driven by self preservation, both as an executive, and when his life is on the line. Having constantly crush the dreams of writers is sure to lead to some enemies. What worries me is that, with the waves of unwatchable garbage that we already have to sift through to find something enjoyable, I can't help but lose sleep imagining what kind of ideas couldn't even get a look in! Soon after being the last straw to an unstable writer, Mill becomes embroiled in a murder mystery in which he himself is the main suspect. Opening with a nine minute single shot sequence, it’s immediately clear that director Robert Altman is crafting something brilliant. Choreographing a series of actors, a camera, a crew, as well as hundreds of extras is not only wildly impressive, but is the heart and soul of directing a film. Blocking a scene from start to finish allows the audience to witness the dynamic nature of a well written scene, be that a change in a single character's emotion, or a shift of power or control between a pair. Altman uses this long take to make the world of the film feel lived in, as if each character is only a small piece of the puzzle in the lively yet mysterious world of Hollywood. The Player is jam packed with cameos from real Hollywood stars improvising at the top of their game and a script laced with dark comedy, leaving you with some genuine and unexpected laughs. Tim Robbins’ paranoid performance was delivered to perfection and was a contributing factor in the slow burn thriller staying engaging and deserving of a 4/5.
