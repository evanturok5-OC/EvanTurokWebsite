Movie review - Singin' in the rain Written by Evan Turok Donen, Kelly, and Charlston: two are directors and one is a dance move, but all three were needed to make Singin’ in the Rain (1952) the masterpiece it is today. Telling the story of silent movie stars Don Lockwood (Gene Kelly) and Linda Lamont (Jean Hagen) transitioning into the revolutionary world of talking pictures. Unfortunately for Lamont, her singing voice isn't up to scratch, so Lockwood is forced to bring in the lovely Kathy Selden (Debbie Reynolds) to sing in the background, void of any credit. Singin’ in the Rain is one you may not have seen, but you have certainly seen its influence in countless other films. Musicals like La La Land and The Greatest Showman can thank Singin’ in the Rain for musical conventions like elaborate, deeply layered dance numbers, often using many choreographed extras to fill the frame with Golden Age Hollywood magic. What I love about the influence of Singin’ in the Rain is that it opened the gates for modern musicals to play into the fact that they are, in fact, films. Spontaneous dance numbers became commonplace, and beautiful new sets were often stumbled into by actors and used to create whimsical moments that stick with you long after the credits roll. Truly a feel-good film that makes you want to laugh and dance throughout. My jaw was on the floor watching this film, and no, it wasn't because I was trying to do the worm (that hadn't been invented in 1952). But it was because of the editing. By now, you would know that I am a huge fan of smooth editing. I can't get enough of Singin’ in the Rain's long takes and effortless transitions. There are a few scenes I often find myself going back to, studying editing tricks in slow motion, of which I can't seem to work out no matter how hard I look. For a film released in 1952, that’s a true testament to its innovative style. Whether it's the rendition of ‘Time Goes By’ in Casablanca (1942) or the dance number in Napoleon Dynamite (2004), musical scenes are often the best part of a great film. So when a film is filled with those fantastic scenes, it will score a deserved 4.5 stars from me.
