Movie review - Cool Hand Luke Written By Evan Turok The charismatic Paul Newman gives his finest performance in the 1967 film Cool Hand Luke. He plays a man sent to a prison labor camp for a senseless crime. During his time in the prison, his hopeful attitude and ability to find the best in a bad situation inspire the other convicts and make him a target for the brutal warden, hell-bent on maintaining a spirit-crushing order. Cool Hand Luke is a story about brotherhood, learning to lean on one another when things get hard, and being able to enjoy the little things that hold us together. Newman is a master of expressing the full range of emotions, whether it is the teary-eyed grief of losing a loved one or the high-energy excitement that inspires the prisoners, Newman makes the most of every second of screen time. The film's most powerful scene comes when Newman sings a song to honor his late mother. As the passion pours out on the screen, a single tear rolls down his cheek, expressing more raw emotion than words ever could. Using the camera to tell a story is always the first priority when shooting a film. Common cinematic techniques, for example using a massive wide shot with a single person in the frame, are a simple, visual way to show isolation. There is always a straightforward way to tick these storytelling boxes, but director Stuart Rosenberg never takes the easy way out. Shooting scenes through the reflection of a prison guard's sunglasses or interchanging the foreground and background in and out of focus with artistic precision are just some of the ways this film stands out visually and holds your attention. My goal has always been to inspire readers to watch movies, and part of that is avoiding diving too deep into the endings of a film. But in this case, you have had 59 years to see Cool Hand Luke—this one is on you! The film's devastating ending was not only ahead of its time but left me absolutely shattered and loving every second of it. Taking bold risks, both visually and story-wise, makes Cool Hand Luke a film that truly sits above the standard. 4/5
