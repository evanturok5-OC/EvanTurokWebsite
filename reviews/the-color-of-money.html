Movie Review - The Color of Money Written by Evan Turok Cinema is exciting when the touch of a director's inimitable style can be seen crafting the shots and using the camera in creative ways, creating memorable scenes that, if nothing else, were certainly ‘cool’. The Color of Money (1986) follows Eddie Felson (Paul Newman) as an experienced pool hustler trying to wrangle in the proud, young, unbeatable player Vincent Lauria (Tom Cruise) and teach him that the way to make money isn't always to win every game. There is also a modern tendency to make a movie colorless. The soulless corporations releasing endless still-camerated grey sludge onto your Netflix accounts is almost enough to make you want to read a book. Don't give up hope just yet, because the works of Martin Scorsese are not yet lost to time. Scorsese puts on a master class in shot styling and coloring a scene. To make a movie colorful, you can't just saturate each frame with every shade and tone under the sun. Giving life to a scene comes from a contrast of colors and sticking to a conscious palette that can act as a canvas to stand behind the rest of the film, accentuating the conflicting colors. The green felt coating a pool table leaks out into the frame but doesn't become plain or repetitive. The dingy, smoke-filled pool halls are hazy but not grey; ironically, The Color of Money earns its name. Moving from gangster heists to the limited scale of small-time pool hustling, outstretches a branch of relatability that drags us deeper into the world of the film, but runs the risk of stripping away that classic Scorsese edge that always keeps me engaged. Unfortunately, the grounded scale offered no crutch to support the film's final scenes, and just like following in the white ball after the black, the ending fell flat. The Color of Money isn't the most revolutionary film of all time, but instead a simple story with memorable scenes and characters with genuine depth and coherently written motivations, all directed by one of the greatest of all time. A fun watch right from the break. 3.5/5
