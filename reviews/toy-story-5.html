Movie Review - Toy story 5 Written By Evan Turok Andrew Stanton’s Toy Story 5 is a story of replacement and the struggles of growing up in today's world, told through a tale of toys vs. technology. When the band of toys now owned by 8 year old Bonnie are suddenly beginning to be forgotten and cast away in place of an iPad, they begin to worry about not only their own abandonment, but also Bonnie's disconnection from herself and the real world around her. The sentient toys spring into action to reignite Bonnie’s imagination and help her make friends with other like-minded kids who still believe in playing rather than gaming. Whether you are a fan of the original or you have been alive recently, I'm sure you have heard of the starring toy, Woody, voiced by Tom Hanks. This cowboy doll, over the past 31 years, has experienced levels of unmatched character development that is nothing short of perfection, so when I heard of a fifth movie, I was terrified, knowing that anything more that could be done to Woody would only serve to ruin the conclusive arc he had already reached. Stanton and co-writer Kenna Harris were on the same page here, choosing to push into centre stage past supporting character, cowgirl toy, Jessie. This genius move allowed for emotional shifts and further development without a forced nature, all the while leaving room for a little sprinkle of the classic characters, who are now graciously and respectfully reduced to side characters. Unfortunately, the side plots taken on by these characters are quite weak and uninteresting, lacking the charm and comedy we have come to love in the Toy Story franchise. Toy Story 5’s message about how technology is forcing kids to grow up too fast is a brutal one, but never more relevant than right now. This film makes a point that friends are people you spend time with and care about, not just people in a group chat. Strong emotion is still abundant five films later. Scenes of online bullying and the replacement of toys all over the neighborhood are gut-wrenching in that signature Pixar style. A decent film, but please, leave this franchise alone now.
