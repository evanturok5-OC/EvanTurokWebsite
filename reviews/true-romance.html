My review

Movie review - True Romance Written by Evan Turok My favorite director of all time, Quentin Tarantino, writer/director of some of the most popular box office hits of this generation. Pulp Fiction and Kill Bill are just a few titles from his blemishless resume, but even the greats have to start somewhere. In 1987, Tarantino wrote ‘True Romance’ and sold the script to director Tony Scott to help fund his future films. This detachment from Tarantino's name recognition left True Romance as one of the most underrated and overlooked films I have ever seen. True Romance follows lovers Clarence Worley (Christian Slater) and Alabama Whitman (Patricia Arquette), who find themselves in possession of half a million dollars' worth of drugs. Upon deciding they need the money, they set off to sell it to a rich movie producer, all the while being hunted down by the police, a Russian mob, and the previous owners of the drugs, all culminating in an explosive climax at the end of the film. It's not often you watch a film and actually care about a character on a personal level. True Romance’s dialogue is so smooth and naturalistic that it humanises the protagonists; you begin to worry for them as if they were real. Of course, you want to see the bad guys catch up with the loving couple, or else what's the point of the film? But the closer they get, the more I begin to sit up straighter and lean forward. Before I knew it, I was hovering 10 centimeters off the couch, holding my breath in anticipation of the final showdown. Underrated is really the best word to describe everything about this film. The iconic Gary Oldman delivers a very unsettling performance as the murderous drug dealer Drexl Spivey. The cunning methodical tone he speaks with before lashing out is reminiscent of a hungry crocodile waiting calmly, with its eyes above the waterline, preparing to strike. Like a great song, there is a smooth rhythm to this film that demands your full attention and hits the beats of a well-rounded story with an effortless grace. If there's ever a film I can confidently return to, assured that it will hold up, True Romance is there waiting, with a satisfying 4.5/5.
